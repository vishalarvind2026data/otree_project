Scientific Purpose & Debriefing

The goal of this research study is to understand how policy framing shapes commuting decisions in urban and suburban regions.

Specifically, this experiment investigated whether a financial loss (a carbon tax surcharge on fossil-fuel cars) or an equivalent financial gain (a clean mobility voucher discounting public transit) is more psychologically effective in promoting modal shift.

Strict Economic Equivalence: In both policy conditions (Treatment B: Carbon Surcharge and Treatment C: Clean Voucher), the net monthly cost advantage of public transit over private car was calibrated to be exactly identical (+€30 per month) relative to the baseline control condition. Only the psychological framing differed.
